What changes—and what does not

Every run loses raw points under every tested setting. These formulas remove substantial original combo rewards without replacing the old point budget. Lower totals are a scale change, not evidence of worse play. Relative ranks and same-wave comparisons below show who benefits relative to peers.

Original scoring favors the better-protected recording in 11 of 12 strict loss-differing pairs. Player-only combo keeps that count. Reference per-shot rewards and bounded contribution each favor it in 8 of 12. Those pairs share recordings and cover only waves one–two; there are no strict matches after wave four.

User clarification: survival already rewards better defense through continued play. Scoring should reward better skill. Protection comparisons below are context, not a test of which formula best measures skill; that criterion has not yet been defined well enough to select a winner.

The three hypotheses

All retain every scored kill's base value, existing wave/building survival bonuses and original debits. Automated kills include upgrades and EMP/F-15; impact-origin kills are also non-manual. No new save bonus is inferred.

A · Player-only combo

base + survival + manual multi-kill awards + α × manual combo uplift

Original combo timing and miss resets remain. Automation receives base only. This removes the player's ability to boost automated rewards, but a miss can still reduce future manual rewards. Settings: α = 0.5, 1, 1.5.

B · Per-shot rewards

base + survival + P per productive shot + M per extra kill (up to 3)

No persistent streak. Include observed chains; pay each increment at kill time, including earned kills from unresolved terminal shots. Empty or contested empty shots have no direct points or penalty. Settings (P,M): (25,50), (50,100), (100,200).

C · Defense plus bounded contribution

base + survival + min(manual damage credit, c × all kill base), per wave

Credit each destroyed target's base proportionally to observed applied damage. This can credit assists without requiring the last hit. Cap settings: 25%, 50%, 100% of the wave's kill base. Existing survival points reward progression/buildings; they do not comprehensively measure Burj, launcher or site protection.

Accounting only: no candidate score enters the simulation or shop. Gross earnings and net after original spending/penalties are separate. This corpus has zero recorded debits, so the two agree here. Fractions remain unrounded in calculations.

These are algebraic comparisons at reference settings, not altered-input replays. No shots were added or removed. The four-kill cap in B makes splitting a large cluster lucrative on paper, despite rewarding a four-kill shot over four singles. Empty spam pays nothing directly, but B supplies no score risk for speculative shots and still rewards unnecessary successful ones. Ammo and timing costs continue to matter.

C removes the last-hit discontinuity when the damage history is unchanged, but still rewards manual damage that automation might otherwise have delivered. Its reference cap saturates in 123/194 wave records, including terminal waves. Further manual contribution then earns no extra premium at fixed total base value. That can tolerate sensible reliance on defense, but can also remove encouragement to keep contributing.

The recordings contain 508 empty shots whose intended targets were killed by another source during the shot lifetime. This overlap is not proof of unfair stealing or wasted play. Observed manual assist credit on non-manual kills is 0; C's proposed assist advantage is therefore supported by rule cases, not a demonstrated benefit in this corpus.

Decision boundary

A directly addresses the automated-combo incentive but preserves risk from empty manual shots. B removes streak risk but rewards unnecessary kills and splitting larger groups. C bounds the contribution premium and avoids a pure last-hit rule, yet often saturates and has no observed assist benefit here.

Do not choose a replacement from these recordings. The next design decision is which skilled choices and execution should earn more when both runs survive. Timing, aim, prioritization and resource use are candidate dimensions to clarify, not validated metrics. More manual contribution or fewer asset losses alone cannot settle that question. Part Six and late-wave experiments have not begun.
