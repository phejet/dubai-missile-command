19 September 2026 · RM-09 follow-up · Local implementation

Bombs now fly toward the roof they select

Both waypoint and legacy carrier drops use one launch calculation. Falling speed remains randomly sampled from 2.4–4.0 pixels per tick. Horizontal speed now covers the distance to the target in the time remaining until roof height.

timeToRoof = (roofY − spawnY) / vy
vx = (targetX − spawnX) / timeToRoof

Target selection is unchanged. Releases without a live building, or with a selected roof at or above the release point, are skipped rather than creating an invalid upward trajectory.

1,238 / 1,238 recorded launch scenarios hit and destroyed their selected building in isolated real simulation. All 132 previously misaligned paths are corrected. Every scenario preserves its original vertical speed and the two existing RNG draws for target selection and speed.

Verification

  • Seven regression cases cover both drop paths, the recorded wrong-building hit, left/right approaches, falling-speed samples and missing/unreachable roofs.
  • 227 focused simulation, headless determinism and replay tests pass; the seed-42 canary remains unchanged.
  • 21 maintained browser smoke/replay checks pass, including portrait layout and wave seeking.
  • TypeScript, focused ESLint, formatting and diff checks pass.

The launch experiment disables other assets and defenses to isolate arrival at the selected building. It is a counterfactual geometry/collision check, not a claim that all those bombs would hit in normal play. Interception, diversion, an intervening asset or earlier target destruction can still prevent an intended hit.

Replay compatibility

The simulation change advances replay compatibility from v11 to v12. Old v11 captures are rejected by this build rather than replayed with different trajectories. Their original data and the historical audit remain intact. All three maintained performance replay fixtures were re-recorded; the wave-four fixture contains changed aiming actions. Performance baselines were not recaptured and should not be compared across the changed fixture as though the workload were identical.

Ready to feel-check

Watch bombs released off to one side of a surviving building: their fall should line up with its roof rather than drift into the neighboring building. Building pressure may increase because previously incorrect trajectories now connect.

The separate between-tick collision defect is unchanged. No gameplay tuning, commit or deployment is included.
