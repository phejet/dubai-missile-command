City buildings survive mostly because threats never reach them. Two real defects also exist.

This audit covers the ten surrounding buildings. The main tower is excluded. All 26 saved human runs reproduce exactly, and the observer preserves their simulation state.

What targets the skyline?

Every one of the 1238 dropped bombs selects a live building’s roof center. Ordinary missiles, MIRVs and stack missiles select the tower, launchers or defense sites instead. They can still hit city buildings incidentally. The selector’s “70%” means nearest versus second-nearest target; it is not a building-targeting percentage.

Including carrier drones, bombs represent 19.1% (1238/6479) of all spawned hostile entities. These counts include each spawned descendant once: 4485 root entities and 1994 descendants. A stack parent changing type is not counted again. Bombs are counted at drop, not when their carrier spawns; intercepted carriers therefore reduce actual bomb exposure.

Only 18 bombs destroyed their intended building; one destroyed a different building. Of the 19 resolved bombs arriving at a building while their selected target remained alive, 18 hit the intended target (94.7%). That conditional figure excludes 1149 intercepted/neutralized bombs, 69 still alive at recording end, and one ground impact after its target had already died. It must not be read as general bomb accuracy.

“Player-caused” is the explosion’s recorded ownership flag, including inherited chains; “non-player” includes defense and impact-origin blasts. These categories do not establish that the player deliberately defended a building, or that every interception prevented a future hit. Flare outcomes and direct defense damage are recorded at their actual destruction branches.

Defect 1: the bomb selects a target but its velocity does not reliably reach it

At drop, vx = (targetX − startX) × 0.004, while vy is independently randomized between 2.4 and 4.0. Both stay constant in normal bomb flight. Horizontal arrival therefore depends on drop height and vertical speed; storing targetX / targetY does not guide the bomb.

At the roof, x = startX + vx × (roofY − startY) / vy. 132 initial forward rays never intersect the selected building’s rectangle. Most of those bombs are intercepted first; the geometric defect is not 132 observed failed impacts.

Defect 2: endpoint-only collision can miss a crossing

run-003, wave 7, warhead 194, tick 5850: a MIRV child travels from (291.47, 1234.26) to (299.29, 1278.92). The segment crosses building 4, but its endpoint lies beyond the right edge. The collision checks only that endpoint, so the building survives. The warhead is intercepted two ticks later. This was an incidental building crossing by a threat aimed at a defense site, not a building-targeted bomb.

Across all verified human runs, 52 eligible endpoint contacts destroyed their buildings: zero damage-application failures. An independent swept-segment check finds one additional missed crossing (1/53 observed geometric contacts, 1.9%). No swept-only bomb contact occurs. The actual missed missile segment reproduces in an isolated real update; a separate deliberately high-speed fixture also demonstrates the general endpoint limitation.

Why survival feels generous

The 26 runs start with 260 building instances and end with 200 still alive: 76.9% survival, or 7.7 of 10 buildings per run on average. This mixes different run lengths and is descriptive, not a balance target. All 60 losses reconcile to an actual threat: 52 missile/bomb collisions and eight drone dive impacts.

Coverage and verification

Scanned the repository’s local JSON replay payloads, including nested diagnostics, saved runs, Staging backups, public/performance fixtures and prior study inputs: 110 payloads, 89 unique replay signatures. Overlapping exports are deduplicated using seed, actions, initial state, mode, endpoint, version and build.

  • 26 saved human runs: verified checkpoints, endpoint, actions and full operator summaries.
  • One automated recording also verifies, but contains no spawned threats and contributes no gameplay measurements.
  • 50 automated recordings diverge from the current simulator and are quarantined. They are not silently treated as valid historical evidence.
  • 12 legacy fixtures lack a recorded final tick and cannot establish a verified historical endpoint.

All 3,159 human checkpoints and 4,819 sampled full-state/RNG hashes agree between unmodified and observed playback. Four controlled collision cases pass, and both replay-derived defects reproduce independently of player input. Input simulation files were not changed; instrumentation is applied only to generated analysis bundles.

This is exhaustive for the local payloads discovered, including the 26-session Staging snapshot previously retrieved on September 15. No operator bearer is available in this session, so the live server inventory was not refreshed. Newer, expired or deleted recordings are not covered. Checkpoint agreement supports current-code reconstruction; it cannot prove every unrecorded historical intermediate state.

Recommended next change

  1. Make bomb launch velocity geometrically consistent with its selected roof target, preserving a deliberate speed/fall-time policy. Re-measure building pressure afterward; this will change replay outcomes.
  2. Use swept collision for missile/bomb building intersections, with explicit first-contact and collision-order rules. Keep the tower outside this investigation’s scope.
  3. Only then decide whether building-directed threat frequency needs tuning. The present recordings already show substantial incidental protection.
