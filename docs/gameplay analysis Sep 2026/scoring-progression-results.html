What this answers

  • Progression is a strong predictor of final score. A simple final-wave line fits 90.9% of score variation here; leave-one-run-out predictive R² is 87.9%. This is not a causal allocation of score to survival versus skill.
  • Some early-wave evidence favors cleaner defense. In 11 of 12 strict pairs with unambiguously different loss outcomes, the better-protected run earns more. But all 12 are waves one–two, share runs, and retain different seeds and starting combos.
  • Protection and score can disagree. One matched wave-two run loses no assets yet earns 704 fewer points than a peer losing two Burj HP. Starting combo and encounter differences remain plausible explanations.
  • The late-wave question remains open. These recordings do not isolate shooting, build quality or active timing at the reported difficulty wall. No wave-11 recording exists.

Keep score as a mixed outcome measure for now. This study does not validate it as a ranking of defensive quality, and does not justify choosing a replacement scoring formula.

What evidence would settle the remaining question?

  • Late-wave attempts starting from the same encounter, loadout, asset health and combo, with the player responding to that encounter. Historical shot coordinates cannot fairly stand in for a player reacting to changed spawns.
  • Enough repetitions with different protection outcomes to distinguish timing/aiming effects from seed and build effects.
  • Separate accounting for observed asset damage, shooting, upgrade kills and active use. A target being intercepted low is still not proof that the manual shot saved it.

Part four is complete as an observational analysis. It identifies the available signal and the missing comparisons. No new experiment, scoring change or part-five rescoring has been run.

Verification and limits

26 context replays match recorded checkpoints, exact final ticks, action consumption and maintained stored-summary comparisons. 168 completed-wave rows + 26 terminal rows reconcile to the prior event ledger and final scores. Wave-start/end balances match completed-wave earnings.

13 statistical checks cover correlation/ties, linear fit, leave-one-out prediction, within-group centering, partial-order comparisons and quantiles. An independent Python calculation agrees on the progression fit, matching coverage and all 40 stratified associations. Strict examples pass exact matching and loss-order assertions.

Uncertainty, terminology and reproduction

Within-group correlations center both score and outcome separately within each tier's matching groups. Run-block resampling draws whole runs with replacement, preserving their waves together; one thousand draws use seed 16092026. Groups need at least two distinct original runs in each resample. Undefined correlations are omitted and valid counts reported. The 2.5–97.5 percentile range is sampling sensitivity for this small one-player corpus, not a population skill confidence interval. Leave-one-run-out ranges remove a whole recording at a time.

Asset-loss counts omit harmless arrivals, invulnerability-blocked hits and hypothetical future threats. The building bonus mechanically favors surviving buildings; total and combat-only results are therefore shown separately. Shot success shares kill outcomes with score and is not an independent assessment of skill. Nine clip labels are too sparse and their individual-action scope too uncertain to assign to entire waves.
