02 / The current incentive

You build the multiplier.
Everything spends its value.

Successful player blasts raise the shared combo, up to ×10. An empty resolved player blast resets it to ×1. Both player kills and automated kills receive the multiplier.

At ×10, holding fire can protect future automated rewards.
A successful shot still earns points, but cannot raise combo further. An empty shot risks reducing future rewards from your whole defense network. Automated kills do not consume combo; the problem is who carries the risk.
Full scoring rules relevant to the study

Kill points = threat base value × shared combo

Base values: ordinary missile 28; drones 20/40; MIRV 100; bomb 42; MIRV warhead and stack2 56; stack3 72.

Root multi-kills award 150 / 350 / 700 for two / three / four-plus kills. The points are not restricted to player explosions. Wave completion adds 250 × wave; surviving buildings add 100 × buildings × wave. A direct friendly F-15 hit costs 500.

Paid shop purchases use the same score balance. These 26 recordings use draft mode, so paid spending must not be assumed to explain their differences.

The displayed hit ratio counts automated kills too. It cannot independently tell us how accurately you shot.
03 / The difficulty wall

More decisions.
Not just more simultaneous deadlines.

Your experience: around waves 10–11, builds and active weapons seem to matter more than execution. The code increases late-wave threat budget and concurrent pressure, but we have not yet measured whether particular losses were avoidable through better timing.

Problem hypothesis

Pressure overlaps until choices disappear

When too many threats require answers at once, accuracy may no longer compensate for insufficient firing capacity.

Concept sketch, not measured replay data.
Experiment to test

Stagger pressure to reward timing

Keep dangerous encounters, but test distinct bursts, viable cluster shots and enough recovery time for timing to change the outcome.

Concept sketch, not a tested new spawn schedule.
The question to measure: with the same build, could a better timed sequence of shots materially change survival? Separate missed opportunities from weak coverage and genuine capacity overload.
04 / Candidate experiments

Three scoring ideas. No winner yet.

These are hypotheses to compare against the same recorded events. No alternative scoring model has been run yet.

Idea What the player gains What could go wrong Evidence needed
Player-only combo bonus
Automation earns base value.		 Precision directly improves the reward for your own shots. Last-hit competition; contested misses still feel unfair. Who gains points at matched waves, and whether useful continued shooting pays.
Per-shot quality bonus
Reward productive shots and clusters.		 A good shot has its own value without risking the whole defense multiplier. Cheap single-kill spam may beat careful multi-kills. Shot efficiency, cluster quality, wasted coverage and sensitivity to timing.
Defense score + player contribution
Keep survival value; add bounded shooting and assist rewards. 		Build strategy and hands-on execution both count. Attribution becomes opaque; “save” bonuses may reward imaginary rescues. Reliable kill/assist ownership and understandable feedback. Causal proof for saves.

Design target: exceptional shooting earns a meaningful premium under comparable conditions. We do not need to force an early wave to score higher than a later one.

05 / How we reach a decision

Replay first. Change the rules second.

Inventory and reproduce Done

26 retained Staging sessions downloaded. All replay checkpoints, stored summaries and final ticks agree.

Attribute every reward

Record player shots, automated kills, active abilities, chain roots, assists and score changes. Reconcile the event ledger to the original score exactly.

Judge shooting without seeing score

Review 8–12 short clips: cluster shots, misses, contested kills, sensible restraint and urgent threats. Compare equal-wave opportunities, builds and completed versus partial waves.

Rescore the same battle

Calculate candidate points beside the unchanged simulation. Preserve purchases, damage, RNG and original replay verification. Hold out entire runs and check whether results survive different parameter choices.

Measure and test pressure separately

Align impact deadlines, firing charges, recharge gaps, live defenses and active availability. Test staggering and composition with controlled threat budgets. Changed spawns need new responses—not blindly reused shot coordinates.

Choose finalists, then feel-check

Show who gains or loses points and why. Test whether the player feels rewarded for useful shooting while sensible reliance on upgrades still works.

What we can—and cannot—claim.

Method and source notes

One player's 26 iOS draft-mode runs across eight builds; 168 completed waves and 26 fatal partial waves. Final waves range from 2 to 10. No wave-11 recording is available. These are repeated observations across different builds and seeds, not population-wide evidence.

Highest-score counts include ties. One run only completes wave 1; excluding it yields 22/25 with current scores and 20/25 after bonus subtraction. The second comparison includes completed waves only. The correlation is Pearson's r, pooled across builds; it does not establish causation.
